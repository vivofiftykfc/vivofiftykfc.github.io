Fuzzy Comprehensive Evaluation (FCE)

Fuzzy Comprehensive Evaluation

Quantities may be divided into two broad classes: certainty and uncertainty.

  • Certainty is studied by classical mathematics, including geometry and algebra.
  • Uncertainty includes:
    1. Randomness, which is studied through probability theory and stochastic processes. Throwing a die and observing which face appears is a familiar example: probability and statistics are used to describe the regularities behind such phenomena.
    2. Greyness, which is studied through grey system theory.
    3. Fuzziness, which is studied through fuzzy mathematics. Expressions such as “the weather is hot today” and “the young man is handsome” belong to this category.

Fuzzy comprehensive evaluation is an evaluation method grounded in fuzzy mathematics. On the basis of membership theory, it converts qualitative judgments into quantitative ones. In other words, fuzzy mathematics is used to reach an overall assessment of an object constrained by several factors. The method produces relatively clear results and has a strong systematic character. It is particularly useful for vague questions that resist direct quantification and is therefore suitable for many problems involving uncertainty.

Basic Concepts of Classical and Fuzzy Sets

Classical sets and characteristic functions

  • A classical set is a collection of things sharing a common property, such as a colour, a sex, a mobile-phone brand, or membership in the natural numbers.
  • The basic properties of such a set are exclusiveness and certainty—the conception of sets that we have generally accepted since high school.

Fuzzy sets and membership functions

  • A fuzzy set describes a vague concept, such as handsome, tall, fair-skinned, or young.

  • Unlike a classical set, a fuzzy set permits something to belong and not belong at the same time, at least to different degrees. It therefore lacks strict certainty and mutual exclusiveness. Many real situations are of this kind, including Problem B in the first university modeling contest of 2025.

  • In mathematics, a fuzzy set is represented by a membership function:

    $$
    \mathbf{u_A: U \rightarrow [0,1]}
    $$
    The distinction between the binary set ${0, 1}$—that is, ${0, 1}$—and the interval $[0, 1]$ matters here. The former permits only two possibilities, whereas the latter contains infinitely many.

Methods for Determining a Membership Function

Fuzzy statistics

One may construct the values oneself or collect them through a questionnaire.

Existing objective scales

For example, the Engel coefficient may be used to describe a household’s level of wealth.

Assignment methods

Certain distributions are selected directly as membership functions according to the nature of the problem. This approach is highly subjective.

Among these choices, trapezoidal distributions are used relatively often.

Application: Fuzzy Comprehensive Evaluation

An outline of the evaluation problem

A fuzzy evaluation problem generally attempts to accomplish one of two tasks:

① Assign an object in the universe of discourse to a specified comment in the evaluation set.
② Treat the available schemes as the evaluation set and select the best scheme.

Three sets are introduced in fuzzy comprehensive evaluation. Note that the symbols below do not necessarily carry the same meanings as similar symbols elsewhere.

  • Factor set (evaluation-index set) $U = {u_1,u_2,…,u_n}$
  • Evaluation set (set of possible results) $V = {v_1,v_2,..,v_m}$
  • Weight set (weights of the indicators) $A = {a_1,a_2,…,a_n}$

Single-level fuzzy comprehensive evaluation

  1. Determine the factor set

    In a single-level evaluation, n is usually small, generally no greater than 5, and the indicators are not strongly correlated.

  2. Determine the evaluation set

  3. Determine the weight of each factor

    Weights can be obtained in many ways, including the Delphi method—an expert survey, or, less ceremoniously, an exercise in making up numbers—the weighted-average method, and collective assessment. A practical suggestion is to use the analytic hierarchy process when no data are available and the entropy weight method when data do exist.

  4. Construct the fuzzy comprehensive judgment matrix and evaluate each element

  5. Carry out the fuzzy comprehensive judgment

Multi-level fuzzy comprehensive evaluation

A single-level evaluation forms the basis of a multi-level one. Indicators that are related to one another are first combined within a group. As noted above, the factors directly compared at a single level should not be strongly correlated; within the scope of that level, they may therefore be treated as relatively independent. Each related group is then combined to obtain its degree of membership in the evaluation set. The same operation is repeated at higher levels until an overall result is produced.

Summary

  1. There are three principal ways to determine a membership function, and the choice should depend on the actual problem.
  2. Benefit-type and cost-type indicators are different concepts from the three kinds of fuzzy sets—left-shoulder, intermediate, and right-shoulder—and should not be confused.
  3. The factor indicators in a single fuzzy comprehensive evaluation should not be strongly correlated. Strongly related items require a multi-level evaluation, which usually contains no more than three levels.
