06-09 Thinking and Building

This thought probably came to me, for no obvious reason, while I was reading a manga adapted from a novel. The original author likes to work his own experience into the story, so the book reveals something of his views on love, his philosophy of life, and all those small amusements that belong to a relationship.

That sent me down quite a long train of thought. Personally, I do want to meet more people at this stage of my life, and perhaps get into a relationship. Yet the more I think about it, the more I notice a serious problem: whenever I talk to someone, we rarely seem to meet on the same wavelength. Sometimes the conversation becomes nothing more than my one-sided complaining; at other times, we simply sit there in awkward silence. I suspect there are two sides to the problem—conversational skill, and either the breadth of one’s knowledge or the capacity to offer an interesting point of view.

As for conversational skill, I may begin with something I once came across online. Da Bing was speaking to a young man over a livestream, and the man had exactly my problem: he never got to the point, filled his speech with needless words, and expressed himself poorly. The advice he received was to speak less. Before answering, pause for perhaps two or three seconds—no more than five—to organise and pare down what you are about to say. If that is not enough, simply tell the other person that you need a moment to think. Remove what is unnecessary, then offer the shortest statement that still captures the point.

Two ideas lie behind this advice. First, if you already know that speaking is not your strength, then instead of worrying that you may leave something unsaid, you might as well respect your present limitations: the more one talks, the more likely one is to go wrong. Second, refining a sentence is itself a form of intellectual training, through which you learn to explain an idea both briefly and clearly. There is an additional benefit, too. The other person is likely to feel respected, because you are visibly taking their words seriously enough to think before responding.

I can leave the other techniques for another day. This is something for which I should deliberately seek out material, practise more often, hold more worthwhile conversations, discuss subjects with a higher information density, and do some genuine brainstorming.

Then there is the substance of a conversation—the views one holds and the things one can actually talk about. On the one hand, I should waste less time on fragmented entertainment. An hour of short videos is worse than an hour of gaming; at least finishing a game or watching an anime means putting some time into an interest of my own. On the other hand, I should look for material that contains thought: organise some of the highly rated answers on Zhihu, read more books, and learn more skills instead of letting my brain rot all day. I have even considered setting aside some free time each week to trace the underlying logic of those popular Zhihu answers, although that material may no longer be suitable for this blog.

Sleep.
