A Short Note on the Historical Development

In broad terms, Microsoft first released the Office suite—Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and similar programs. These applications ran locally, and collaboration meant passing files back and forth by hand. In the 2010s, Microsoft introduced Office 365 and added cloud services such as OneDrive and email. In the 2020s, the brand was renamed Microsoft 365, with further collaborative and AI functions.

The appropriate office software therefore depends mainly on how much collaboration is required and how much the user can afford.

The more detailed account below comes from DeepSeek-R1:

A Timeline of Microsoft’s Office Suites

1. The era of perpetual licenses, 1989–2010

  • 1989: Microsoft first proposed the Microsoft Office brand, packaging several previously separate office applications for sale.
  • 19 November 1990: the company released the landmark Office suite for Windows, containing Word 1.0, Excel 2.0, and PowerPoint 2.0. The familiar trio established a standard for document processing that would last for decades.
  • 1995: distributed alongside Windows 95, Office 95 spread rapidly and became the de facto standard for businesses and individuals worldwide, defeating competitors such as WordPerfect and Lotus.
  • The 2000s: Microsoft successively released perpetual-license editions including Office 2000, XP, 2003, 2007, and 2010. Their functions evolved, but they remained locally installed software, and collaboration continued to depend on file transfer.

2. The transition to cloud subscriptions: Office 365, 2011–2019

  • 28 June 2011: Microsoft formally launched Office 365, marking the transition from perpetual licenses to subscription-based cloud services. The first version targeted business and educational users and combined Exchange Online, SharePoint Online, and Lync Online, later renamed Skype for Business.
  • 2013:
    • A premium subscription was introduced for small businesses.
    • The business-intelligence tool Power BI was integrated with Excel.
  • March 2014: subscription plans for individual consumers, including Personal and Family editions, extended cloud services to the consumer market for the first time.
  • 2017:
    • Office 365 accounted for more than half of Office revenue for the first time, a major milestone in the cloud transition.
    • In July, Microsoft launched Microsoft 365 for enterprise, combining Office 365 Enterprise, Windows 10 Enterprise, and the Enterprise Mobility and Security suite. At this point, however, Office 365 remained the principal consumer brand.

3. A unified brand and an expanded ecosystem: Microsoft 365, 2020 onward

  • 30 March 2020: Microsoft announced that the consumer Office 365 subscriptions, Personal and Family, would be formally renamed Microsoft 365 on 21 April. The change marked the retreat of the “Office” brand and the growing importance of the cloud ecosystem.
    • Principal additions: AI-related functions such as Microsoft Editor, Presenter Coach, and Money in Excel.
    • Prices remained unchanged: 398 yuan per year for the Personal edition and 498 yuan for the Family edition in China.
  • October 2022: at its Ignite conference, Microsoft announced that the Office applications on mobile, web, and desktop platforms would all be renamed the Microsoft 365 App, accompanied by revised interfaces and icons.
  • From 2023 onward:
    • AI integration, including Copilot, cross-platform compatibility, and security updates continued to deepen.
    • Microsoft 365 developed into an integrated platform for office work, collaboration, cloud storage, AI, and security, extending well beyond the role of the traditional Office toolkit.

The Timeline at a Glance

Period Principal product or brand Important events Model and characteristics
1990–2010 Microsoft Office The three core applications appeared in 1990; Office spread with Windows 95; perpetual editions were updated Local installation and largely closed functions
2011–2019 Office 365 Launched for enterprises in 2011; expanded to individuals in 2014; more than half of revenue by 2017 Subscription cloud service with online collaboration tools
2020 onward Microsoft 365 Consumer rebranding in April 2020; application-wide rebranding in 2022; deeper AI integration Cloud, AI, and an ecosystem platform across devices

In any case, for my present self—a university student who does not know much yet—the simple answer is to keep using an educational edition of the Office suite.
