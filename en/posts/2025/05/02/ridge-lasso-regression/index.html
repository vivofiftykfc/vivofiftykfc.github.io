Ridge and Lasso Regression

A Complete Introduction to Ridge and Lasso Regression

I. Why Is Regularized Regression Necessary?

1. The difficulty faced by OLS

Ordinary least squares is a fundamental method of modeling. When the data contain multicollinearity or simply too many variables, however, OLS faces two major problems:

  • Unstable coefficient estimates: a minor change in the data may cause a dramatic change in the coefficients.
  • Risk of overfitting: the model performs well on its training set but generalizes poorly.

2. The introduction of regularization

A penalty term is added to the loss function in order to restrict the complexity of the model:

  • Ridge regression: L2 regularization
  • Lasso regression: L1 regularization

II. Mathematical Principles: From OLS to Regularization

1. OLS regression

The loss function is:
$$
\min_{\beta} \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \beta_0 - \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j x_{ij})^2
$$

2. Ridge regression

An L2-norm, or Euclidean-norm, penalty is added, with λ controlling the strength of the penalty:
$$
\min_{\beta} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \beta_0 - \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j x_{ij})^2 + \lambda \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j^2 \right]
$$

Features:

  • All coefficients are shrunk proportionally, but none is reduced exactly to zero.
  • Multicollinearity is alleviated, and model stability improves.
  • Ridge regression addresses an ill-conditioned matrix at the cost of unbiasedness and some precision.

3. Lasso regression

An L1, or Manhattan-norm, penalty is added:
$$
\min_{\beta} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \beta_0 - \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j x_{ij})^2 + \lambda \sum_{j=1}^p |\beta_j| \right]
$$

Features:

  • The coefficients of unimportant variables may be reduced to zero, thereby performing variable selection.
  • The method is suitable for high-dimensional data, including cases in which there are more variables than observations.

III. The Central Operation: Selecting the Regularization Parameter λ

1. K-fold cross-validation

  • Procedure: divide the data into K parts. In turn, use K−1 parts for training and one part for validation, and then calculate the average error.
  • Objective: select the value of λ that minimizes validation error.

2. The regularization path

  • Ridge regression: as λ increases, the coefficients gradually approach zero.
  • Lasso: as λ increases, some coefficients abruptly become zero.

V. Points Requiring Attention

1. The data must be standardized

  • Reason: regularization is sensitive to scale, so a variable with a large numerical range may dominate the penalty.
  • Method: use StandardScaler to give each variable a mean of 0 and a variance of 1.

2. How should the coefficients be interpreted?

  • Ridge regression: the sign of a coefficient indicates the direction of a variable’s influence on the target, but its magnitude is affected by λ.
  • Lasso: a nonzero coefficient indicates an important variable, but absolute magnitudes are not directly comparable.

3. Traps in selecting λ

  • Too small: the model approaches OLS and loses the effect of regularization.
  • Too large: all coefficients are excessively compressed, and the model underfits.

VII. Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: When should ridge be used, and when should Lasso be used?

  • Ridge regression: when all variables are important but collinearity is present, as in the analysis of economic indicators.
  • Lasso: when there are many variables and the principal factors must be selected, as in genetic-data modeling.

Q2: What is Elastic Net?

  • Mixed regularization: L1+L2, balancing variable selection against stability.
  • Formula:
    $$\text{损失函数} + \lambda (\alpha \sum |\beta_j| + (1-\alpha) \sum \beta_j^2)$$

Q3: How should λ=0 be interpreted?

  • When λ=0, the model reduces to ordinary OLS regression.
