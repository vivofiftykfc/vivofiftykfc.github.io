Curve-Fitting Algorithms

Curve-Fitting Algorithms

Unlike interpolation, curve fitting does not require a curve to pass through every data point. Instead, it seeks a function, or curve, that minimizes the overall error between itself and all the observations under a specified criterion. Its central purpose is to construct the most suitable mathematical model by fitting the features of existing data. It is widely used for trend prediction, parameter estimation, and similar tasks.

I. The Difference from Interpolation

Method Principal feature Suitable situation
Interpolation The curve must pass through every sample point and may be affected by Runge’s phenomenon. Precise data and a small sample
Curve fitting The curve need not pass through every point; the global optimum minimizes squared errors. Noisy data and the analysis of trends

When there are too many sample points, a high-order interpolation polynomial may oscillate severely. A fitting algorithm based on a low-order function, such as a linear or exponential function, can reflect the trend of the data more steadily.

II. Principles

1. Mathematical model

Suppose the sample points are $(x_i, y_i), i = 1, 2, \ldots, n$ and the fitted curve is $y = kx + b$. The aim is to minimize the sum of squared errors:
$$
\min_{k,b} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2 \quad \text{其中} \quad \hat{y}_i = kx_i + b
$$

2. Solving for the parameters

Taking partial derivatives and setting them equal to zero yields the closed-form solution:
$$
k = \frac{n \sum x_i y_i - \sum x_i \sum y_i}{n \sum x_i^2 - (\sum x_i)^2}, \quad
b = \frac{\sum x_i^2 \sum y_i - \sum x_i \sum x_i y_i}{n \sum x_i^2 - (\sum x_i)^2}
$$

3. Extension

The model may remain linear in its parameters even when it is polynomial in x, as in a quadratic function:
$$
\quad \hat{y}_i = kx_i + kx_i^2 + b
$$

III. Evaluating Goodness of Fit

1. The coefficient of determination $R^2$

  • Total sum of squares (SST): represents the total variation in the data.
    $$ SST = \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \overline{y})^2 $$
  • Sum of squared errors (SSE): represents fitting error.
    $$ SSE = \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2 $$
  • Regression sum of squares (SSR): represents the variation explained by the model.
    $$ SSR = \sum_{i=1}^n (\hat{y}_i - \overline{y})^2 $$

The goodness of fit is:
$$ R^2 = \frac{SSR}{SST} = 1 - \frac{SSE}{SST} $$

  • Since $ R^2 \in [0, 1] $, a value closer to 1 indicates a better fit.
  • Note: $ R^2 $ applies only to models that are linear in their parameters. Thus, $ y = a + bx^2 $ is linear for this purpose because it is linear with respect to the parameters (a,b).

V. Applications

Case 1: Predicting the population of the United States

A Logistic model is used to fit historical data:
$$
x(t) = \frac{x_m}{1 + \left( \frac{x_m}{3.9} - 1 \right) e^{-r(t-1790)}}
$$
Nonlinear least squares are then used to estimate $x_m$ and $r$ and to predict the population trend over the next thirty years.

Case 2: A model of fish growth

Exponential and trigonometric functions are fitted to data on shark length and weight, and the best model is selected by comparing SSE:
$$
\text{体长增长方程：} \quad y = 69.32e^{-0.004x} \quad (R^2 = 0.9704)
$$

VI. Summary

  1. Suitable situations: prefer fitting when the data contain noise, the overall trend must be extracted, or the relation between variables is complicated.
  2. Model selection: give priority to models linear in their parameters so that $R^2$ can be used; for nonlinear models, compare SSE directly.
  3. Recommended tool: MATLAB’s cftool provides a quick way to fit and visualize several kinds of functions.
