ARCH and GARCH Models

ARCH and GARCH Models

I. Why Are Models of Conditional Heteroskedasticity Needed?

1. Typical features of financial data

Financial time series, including stock returns and exchange-rate movements, often exhibit volatility clustering:

  • A period of violent fluctuation is likely to be followed by further violent fluctuation, as during a market crash.
  • A calm period is likewise likely to be followed by calm, as when a market is moving sideways.

A classic example:
During the global financial crisis of 2008, the S&P 500 repeatedly moved by more than 5 percent in a single day, whereas its daily movement in quiet periods was often below 1 percent.

2. Limitations of conventional models

An ARMA model assumes that the variance of its residuals remains constant. It therefore fails to capture volatility clustering, which may lead to:

  • biased risk forecasts;
  • ineffective portfolio optimisation; and
  • inaccurate pricing of derivatives.

II. The ARCH Model: Autoregression of Volatility

1. Definition of ARCH(1)

For a return series ( r_t ), define its conditional variance as:
$$
\sigma_t^2 = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 \varepsilon_{t-1}^2
$$
where ( \varepsilon_t = \sigma_t v_t ) and ( v_t \sim N(0,1) ).

Key constraints:

  • ( \alpha_0 > 0 )
  • ( 0 \leq \alpha_1 < 1 )

2. The ARCH(q) model

Extending the model to q lags gives:
$$
\sigma_t^2 = \alpha_0 + \sum_{i=1}^q \alpha_i \varepsilon_{t-i}^2
$$
The condition ( \sum \alpha_i < 1 ) is required for stationarity.

III. The GARCH Model: Introducing a Memory Effect

1. The GARCH(p,q) model

Bollerslev (1986) added lagged variance terms to the ARCH model:
$$
\sigma_t^2 = \alpha_0 + \sum_{i=1}^q \alpha_i \varepsilon_{t-i}^2 + \sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j \sigma_{t-j}^2
$$
Constraints:

  • ( \alpha_0 > 0 )
  • ( \alpha_i, \beta_j \geq 0 )
  • ( \sum (\alpha_i + \beta_j) < 1 )

2. Why is GARCH generally preferable?

  • Long memory: conditional variance is affected by earlier variances, enabling the model to represent persistent volatility more effectively.
  • Parsimony: GARCH(1,1) can often replace a high-order ARCH model with far fewer parameters.

VI. Common Pitfalls

1. What if the data are not stationary?

  • Difference the series first and construct a combined ARIMA–GARCH model.

2. What if the model does not converge?

  • Check whether all constraints are satisfied.
  • Try another optimisation algorithm, such as BFGS or NM.

3. How should p and q be selected?

  • Begin with GARCH(1,1) and increase the orders gradually.
  • Compare the AIC and BIC values of the candidate models.
