Entropy Weight Method (EWM)

Entropy Weight Method (EWM)

Background

This is a more objective method for assigning weights that appears frequently in mathematical modeling. Because the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) is too subjective—expert scoring is essentially making things up arbitrarily—the entropy weight method is introduced as an alternative.

Intuition

For a set of data indicators, the larger the variance, the smaller the similarity among the data, and thus the greater its contribution. To take an extreme case: if for a given indicator all of its values are identical, then its similarity is maximal, and the weight assigned to this indicator for the purpose of discrimination can be set directly to zero. Entropy is a measure of uncertainty: the greater the uncertainty, the larger the entropy, and the more information it contains. Consequently, the weight of its influence on the final decision should be set higher.

To illustrate with an example from Qingfeng’s mathematical modeling course: suppose Xiao Zhang and Xiao Wang are two high school students. Xiao Zhang is a good student who always gets full marks on final exams, whereas Xiao Wang is a poor student who often fails. In one particular exam, Xiao Zhang again gets full marks—but Xiao Wang also gets full marks. That is highly unusual. Xiao Wang’s result carries a great deal of information, and the corresponding weight should therefore be higher.

Definitions

  • Information Content

    The amount of information is defined as:
    $$
    I(x) = -\ln(p(x))
    $$

  • Information Entropy
    Information entropy is defined as the expected value of the information content:
    $$
    H(X) = -\sum_{i=1}^{n} p(x_i)\ln(p(x_i))
    $$
    When all events are equally likely, entropy reaches its maximum:
    $$
    H(X) = \ln(n)
    $$

Calculation Procedure

The entropy weight method mainly consists of the following three steps:

Data Standardization

Suppose there are objects and indicators, and the raw data form a matrix (which must first be positively oriented):

$$
X = \begin{bmatrix}
x_{11} & x_{12} & \cdots & x_{1m} \
x_{21} & x_{22} & \cdots & x_{2m} \
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \
x_{n1} & x_{n2} & \cdots & x_{nm}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Positive Orientation

  • Requirement: When computing probabilities in the subsequent steps, every element must be non-negative.
  • Treatment: If any negative values are present, a specific method must be applied to standardize them into the [0,1] interval.
Indicator Type Characteristic Example
Benefit indicator (positive) The larger, the better Grades, profit, GDP growth rate
Cost indicator (negative) The smaller, the better Expenses, pollution level, unemployment rate
Intermediate indicator The closer to a certain value, the better PH value in water quality assessment
Interval indicator Best when falling within an interval Body temperature, the content of a certain substance in water
  • Converting cost indicators to benefit indicators:

$$
x_i = \max{x_1, x_2, …, x_i} - x_i
$$

Note: If all elements are positive, the reciprocal can be taken directly.

  • Converting intermediate indicators to benefit indicators:

For an intermediate indicator sequence with the optimal value being $x_{best}$, and $x_{new}$ being the positively oriented benefit indicator, the orientation formula is:

$$
M = \max{|x_i - x_{best}|}
$$

$$
x_{new} = 1 - \frac{|x_i - x_{best}|}{M}
$$

  • Converting interval indicators to benefit indicators:

For an interval indicator sequence with the optimal interval being $x_{best}$, and $x_{new}$ being the positively oriented benefit indicator, the orientation formula is:
$$
M = \max{a - \min{x_i}, \max{x_i} - b}
$$

$$
x_{new} =
\begin{cases}
1 - \frac{a - x}{M}, & x < a \
1, & a \leq x \leq b \
1 - \frac{x - b}{M}, & x > b
\end{cases}
$$

Standardization Methods

  • Method 1 (applicable when there are no negative values):
    $$
    z_{ij} = \frac{x_{ij}}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_{ij}^2}}
    $$

  • Method 2 (when negative values exist):
    $$
    z_{ij} = \frac{x_{ij} - \min{x_{1j}, x_{2j}, \ldots, x_{nj}}}{\max{x_{1j}, x_{2j}, \ldots, x_{nj}} - \min{x_{1j}, x_{2j}, \ldots, x_{nj}}}
    $$

Calculating the Probability Matrix

Starting from the standardized matrix, calculate the proportion of each sample under each indicator to form the probability matrix:

$$
p_{ij} = \frac{z_{ij}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} z_{ij}}
$$
This ensures that the probabilities in each column (for each indicator) sum to 1.

Calculating Information Entropy and Entropy Weights

Calculating Information Entropy

Compute the information entropy:

$$
e_j = -\frac{1}{\ln n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} p_{ij}\ln(p_{ij}) \quad (j=1,2,\ldots,m)
$$
Note: When $p_{ij}=0$, it is stipulated that $\ln(0)=0$.

Here, dividing by $\ln n$ normalizes $e_j$ to the $[0,1]$ interval.

Calculating the Information Utility Value

The information utility value $d_j$ is defined as:
$$
d_j = 1 - e_j
$$
The larger the information utility value, the more information the indicator contains.

Normalizing to Obtain Entropy Weights

Finally, the information utility values for all indicators are normalized to obtain the entropy weights $\omega_j$:
$$
\omega_j = \frac{d_j}{\sum_{j=1}^{m} d_j} \quad (j=1,2,\ldots,m)
$$

Score

$$
score_i = \sum_{j=1}^{m} w_{j}z_{ij} \quad (i=1,2,\ldots,n)
$$

Extensions

  1. Modified TOPSIS Method: The entropy weight method can be used to modify the TOPSIS method, making the decision-making process more objective.
  2. Combining Subjective and Objective Weights: Combining objective weighting (entropy weight method) with subjective weighting can yield a more reasonable weight allocation.
  3. Other Objective Weighting Methods: For example, grey relational analysis can also serve as a method for calculating weights.
  4. Choice of Standardization Method: Different standardization methods will produce different (Z) matrices, so the appropriate method should be chosen according to the specific situation.

Model Summary

  • Step 1: Check whether the input matrix contains negative values and perform any necessary standardization to ensure the data are non-negative.
  • Step 2: Calculate the proportion of each sample under each indicator to construct the probability matrix.
  • Step 3: Use the probability matrix to compute the information entropy for each indicator, then calculate the information utility value, and finally normalize to obtain the weight for each indicator.
