An Unauthorized Structure in the Mind

Love is probably when one person gradually enters the consciousness of another person, and finally a “we” grows between the two people. But when did this “we” become love, and when did it become nothing more than habit, debt, or unwillingness? Only time can give the result, but time is never responsible for writing the standard answer.

〇. Why does a third thing grow between two people?

I finished writing Gears, Industry and Civilization a few days ago, and now I want to write about love again.

It’s not that love and gears have anything in common. To put it bluntly, it probably sounds when both of them are inappropriate. However, if the gear sounds, you can stop it for inspection. If the gear sounds, people usually say “It’s okay” and then go back to sleep.

After falling in love once, I began to doubt many novels.

Of course, the novel is not really a lie. It’s just that stories always like to write about encounters and heartbeats for a long time: looking at each other in the rain, recognizing each other in the crowd, and saying words that only two people can understand one night. As for how two people arrange time, deal with suspicion, how to talk when they are tired, how to face money, distance, body and each other’s bad temper, they are often compressed into the ending sentence “live happily ever after”.

But it’s only after you actually enter a relationship that you realize that “from now on” may be the main text.

Love certainly has romance, attachment, and sexual attraction, but it also has values, desires, power, boundaries, responsibilities, misunderstandings, projections, and reality conditions. It’s not a pure occurrence like a miracle, or just two people sitting down and calculating what each other has to offer.

So I want to ask again from the beginning: Why does one person fall in love with another person? How does love go from a flutter of heart to a shared history between two people?

1. The birth of love

It is impossible for a person to be loved apart from his own attributes

People cannot be loved apart from their own attributes, Kafka said.

The reason why a person is seen by another person always depends on something specific: appearance, voice, body, personality, ability, experience, expression, or a small action. Maybe she looked at you seriously when she spoke, maybe she stopped to wait for someone when everyone was moving forward, or maybe she was sitting there when the sun was shining brightly one day, and you happened to see her.

The so-called “I love your soul” cannot really separate the soul from the body and experience. At the same time, a person completely devoid of appearance, voice, character, history, and social relationships does not exist. The soul must have something concrete to show its face, otherwise it will be a problem for two people to meet anywhere.

But on the other hand, people are not simply the sum of these attributes.

Family, class, education, gender roles, past relationships and hurts all shape a person. But what truly makes up a person is not just what she goes through, but how she responds to those experiences. People who have also been hurt can choose to retaliate, avoid, remain silent, or they can learn to trust again after a long time.

So I would rather say:

Man is not only the result of the shaping of social relations, but also a subject who can respond to these relations and form his own history.

What love loves is exactly this kind of thing: she carries the traces left by the world, but is not completely determined by these traces. She is already someone and continues to be someone at the same time.

There is always “use” in love

This may not sound very romantic, but it probably can’t be avoided.

If you want the other person to eat with you, you are using her time; if you talk to her, you need her attention; if you want her to be loyal, you want to gain stability from the relationship; hugging, sex, care, recognition, belonging, and living together, all of them satisfy certain human needs.

Even “I just wish her the best” may not be entirely selfless. Seeing that she is doing well makes me feel at ease; helping her confirms that I am a person capable of loving others. If the ledger is burned, the ledger does not necessarily cease to exist. It just changed to a more noble cover.

But discovering this does not mean that love is therefore a scam.

The question is never whether I get something from you, but: Do I see you as a person who can satisfy my needs, or do I just see you as a tool to satisfy my needs?

The former recognizes that intimacy necessarily involves mutual need. The latter will immediately degrade the other person from a person to a failed function when she is temporarily unable to provide value.

Mature love is not about declaring “I don’t need you at all.” If it’s really not needed at all, it’s hard to say how intimate it is. It’s closer to:

I do need you and will get a lot from you; but when you can’t satisfy me, you are still you, not something that suddenly breaks.

Desire and love are not the same thing

Desire says: I want to be close to you, to have you, to touch you, to have you stay with me.

Love gradually takes on another layer: I want you to live better for who you are, even if your “better” sometimes doesn’t fully serve me.

But the two cannot be cut cleanly. Without desire, love can easily become a kind of moral appreciation through glass; with only desire, the other person can easily be compressed into an object that satisfies me.

The real difficulty is not to eliminate desire, but to admit that:

My urge to have you is true, and it is also true that you do not belong to me.

Love starts to get troublesome from here. Sigh, it would be great if I were only responsible for my heartbeat.

2. How Love Changes

A relationship does not rise uniformly from ten per cent affection to one hundred per cent, before a prompt suddenly appears: “Congratulations, you have obtained mature love.” Please do not live real life as though it were a galgame!

It is more like passing through several transformations. Both before and after, we call it love, yet what it contains is no longer quite the same.

“I want to be close to you”

There is attraction, curiosity, and a lot of projection at this time.

Part of the person we fall in love with is a real person, and part of the person we fall in love with is an image we make up based on limited information. She understands me, she is different from others, she can heal me, and she may accompany me for a long time - these judgments sometimes come from her, sometimes from the fact that I needed such a person too much at the time.

Attraction is real and so is imagination. It’s just her in my imagination, not necessarily her.

The two people in “Romeo and Juliet” met, fell in love, got married, went into exile, and both died in a very short period of time. The feelings are certainly strong, but they have little chance of experiencing disenchantment over a long relationship. This story proves that love can burn to a high level at once, but it does not prove that high temperature itself is maturity.

Those two in Verona rushed the schedule rather fiercely. Married life had not even entered the calendar before both of them were gone—a little like Liang Shanbo and Zhu Yingtai. Then again, had they actually survived long enough to live as a married couple, perhaps this love story would not exist at all.

“I’m starting to look forward to you”

After repeated contact, the other person begins to enter everyday life.

When something happens, I want to tell her. I pick up my phone and wait for her message. When I reach a place, I wonder whether she would like it. When planning the future, I begin to leave a place for her. Love is no longer merely a momentary feeling; it has become a stable expectation.

Later, shared memories, habits, commitments and future plans gradually accumulated, and the relationship underwent its first real qualitative change.

Originally there was only “I” and “you”, but then “we” appeared.

A decision is no longer just about “do I want to do it?” but also about: Will this damage our trust? Did it violate our agreement? Where will it lead our common future?

This “we” is not really a third person, but it has its own history and weight, and begins to affect the two people in turn.

However, here, some people don’t like the existence of the concept of “we” and feel that this will restrict their freedom. This is a matter of opinion. Just like a popular saying on the Internet, marriage is to use trouble to resist loneliness.

After the charm is gone, love begins for the second time

Real dealings will expose differences sooner or later.

It is impossible for your partner to understand you forever. Your partner is also a human being. He or she will also be tired, selfish, silent, bored, have a life that you cannot participate in, and will disappoint you at some point. In fact, no one is different. If people live a little longer, it will be difficult to always maintain the refined version when they first met.

Disenchantment does not necessarily mean that you no longer love. It may also be the first time love truly escapes projection:

From loving someone who meets my expectations to loving someone who continues to exceed my expectations.

I saw her limitations and allowed her to overturn my judgment of her. Not because she had proven herself perfect, but because I finally got to face a concrete person.

Mechanism, Calling, and Endurance Are Three Different Questions

Many explanations for why love occurs can be found in physical attraction, attachment needs, upbringing and social conditioning. These are mechanisms.

But the mechanism cannot explain another problem: so many people have similar attributes, why is it this person who made me feel bright at a specific moment?

This is closer to a calling. It’s not that I listed the conditions first and then judged her worthy of liking; it was that she first appeared there as a specific person, my heart moved first, and the reasons came to the scene later.

But calling is only responsible for letting people walk over. It is not responsible for teaching people how to get along, let alone how to continue after many years.

When the initial intensity wears off, what really sustains the relationship becomes something less dazzling: reliability, habits, candor, order in living together, and a willingness to respond to the other person on ordinary days.

Put plainly, mechanism explains why ignition is possible; calling makes the fire actually burn; endurance must answer where the next bundle of firewood will come from. Two people cannot embrace the matchbox of their first heartbeat and expect it to keep them warm for a lifetime.

Time will change love, but it will not testify for love

In “Love in the Time of Cholera”, Ariza waited for fifty years, nine months and four days, but experienced more than 600 relationships during the long wait. It doesn’t seem right to say that he doesn’t love Fermina; to say that he has been pure and loyal for more than fifty years, it’s hard not to think it’s a bit abstract.

Time did not write the answer on his face. It just marinates love, desire, obsession and stories about oneself in the same jar, and then opens them together fifty years later.

So what time tests is not only whether love is still there, but also what this love has become over the long years.

Sometimes it’s trust, sometimes it’s responsibility, sometimes it’s habit, and sometimes it’s just unwillingness to admit that I’ve waited for so long. More often than not, several things are mixed together and no one wants to go out first.

Of course, we still see a tendency to only regard the love in literature as love, and to deconstruct real love into symbols such as desire and obsession in reality. Wittgenstein criticized these things, so I won’t go into detail here.

3. The Unauthorized Structure in the Mind

After the relationship deepens, the other person is no longer just a person in the outside world.

She taps into my emotions, identity, daily habits, memories, and future plans. The original “what do I do on the weekend” gradually becomes “what do we do on the weekend”, and the original “where do I go in the future” becomes “where do we go in the future”. When I encounter something, I will even automatically imagine how she will evaluate it before it is my turn to speak. These are also supported from a neuroscience perspective.

In this sense, love does make another person a part of yourself.

But a patch must be made immediately: she is part of my consciousness, which does not mean that she is my possession. She entered my life without losing her independent life.

The pain of breakup

If the other person has participated in my self-structure, breaking up is not just about losing an external object, it is more like a part of the consciousness that was originally maintained by two people and suddenly lost the support of reality.

People will subconsciously want to tell her things, will automatically think of shared experiences when passing a certain place, will continue to imagine how she will respond, and even the grammar of “we” will remain when planning the future.

That person is no longer there, but the position formed for her is still there. This is very much like a phantom limb: it’s not that the body is really missing another piece, but that the consciousness still interprets the world according to the original layout.

Therefore, the recovery of broken love is not to prove that she was never important, nor is it to judge all memories as errors. What really needs to be done is to re-answer: Who am I without this relationship? What life belongs to me? How should the original future be rewritten? Where should she be placed in my life history? In other words, falling out of love is a self-destruction.

Maturity does not mean completely devaluing your ex. It seems that as long as you prove that she is not worth it, you have not lost anything. I personally feel that this approach is quite tasteless. A more honest statement might be:

That relationship was once real, she was once in my life and shaped a part of me, but she is no longer a part of my current life.

Love Brain

There may be a lot of things mixed in the “love brain”: the high level of attention during the love period, the fear of being abandoned, the lack of self-worth, too few fulcrums in life, the hope that the partner will compensate for past deficiencies, and the addiction caused by the other person’s hot and cold mood.

If a person lacks stable friends, study, work, interests and his own order in his life, his partner can easily become the only emotional outlet, source of recognition, future meaning and focus of life at the same time.

If the other party moves slightly, the whole world will sway.

This does not necessarily mean that love is too big, it may just be that it is too empty elsewhere. How can I put it, when there is only one pillar in the house, that pillar does seem to be very important. Mainly because it’s also scared.

There are also some relationships that people cannot let go of, not because they are happy, but because they are extremely unstable. Sometimes intimate, sometimes cold; sometimes committed, sometimes withdrawn. People keep waiting for the next time intimacy will reappear, and then mistake this anxiety for affection.

Therefore, the intensity of pain cannot directly prove the depth of love. There may be love in it, but there may also be lack, unwillingness, competition and the desire to be chosen again.

Allow to be loved

Some people are good at taking care of others, but not very good at taking care of others.

When someone treats you well, your first reaction is not to feel reassured, but to doubt what the other person is up to; when you accept a little help, you immediately think about how to return it in equal value; when your needs are exposed, you feel that you have lost your dignity, as if you are worthy of being loved only if you are complete, strong, and not troublesome to anyone.

But true intimacy requires one to allow oneself a certain degree of dependence, acceptance, and vulnerability. Accepting being loved does not mean handing over life, it just admits: I will be tired, and there are things I can’t do, and I can also let another person help me at certain times.

Of course, being helped does not mean taking off the leg and giving it to the other person on the spot. There is still some scale in between.

4. Distance creates beauty

The most difficult part of love is probably not how to get infinitely close, but that after getting close, you still admit that the other person is not you.

Power in love

Who is more dependent on whom, who is more afraid of losing, who has more money and life resources, who is better at expressing, who determines the rhythm of the relationship, and who has the power to interpret “normal” - these all create asymmetries.

A mature relationship is not about pretending that power does not exist, but about realizing how you can influence the other person and refraining from abusing it. You cannot repeatedly leave the other person to force her to give in because you are more afraid of losing. You cannot declare her feelings as irrational because you are more reasonable. You cannot turn caring into control because the other person relies on you. What is this? This is a fucking scumbag.

In “Othello”, a handkerchief bears the burden of evidence that it should not bear. Suspicion gradually replaces inquiry, and deep love quickly turns into surveillance, judgment and destruction. Othello finally describes himself as a person who does not love wisely but loves too deeply. But it is also obvious that this sentence is beautiful as an epitaph, but not as a defense statement.

Deep love cannot automatically excuse harm. In fact, the more you can influence a person, the more you should know how much weight your actions will leave on her.

Summoning does not confer qualifications

I am moved by a person and want to be close to her, take care of her, and protect her. This feeling can be completely true, but it can only prove that she has special meaning to me. It cannot just say that I have special meaning to her, nor can it prove that I have gained a ticket to her life.

She doesn’t have to accept my love and doesn’t owe me a response. Nor can I claim to know what is good for her better than she does just because I am sincere. Let’s not turn “I’m good for you” into a master key, trying to open all the doors she doesn’t want to open.

In other words:

Love gives me a reason to care about her, but it doesn’t give me the qualification to own her.

She can refuse, she can change, she can leave. The calling was real, it could be real that she no longer needed me. If two things are true at the same time, they will not erase each other.

“We” cannot destroy “me” and “you”

If a relationship only has integration but no independence, it can easily lead to possession, control, symbiosis and emotional kidnapping. If there is only independence and no integration, they may become two people who are ready to retreat at any time. They have a clear calculation and can move smoothly when difficulties arise. “Husband and wife are originally birds in the same forest, and they fly separately when disaster strikes.”

Mature love does not draw a perfect line from the middle, but continues to bear this tension:

You are an important part of me, but not my possession.

Your destiny is related to me, but your life is not determined by me.

I am willing to adjust myself for you, but I will not destroy myself in exchange for you staying.

We form a community, but the community cannot cancel “I” and “you”.

This is not a problem that can be solved once and for all. I thought clearly today, but tomorrow the other party doesn’t reply for two hours, and my mind starts to build scaffolding downstairs again. Theory is one thing, but actually standing in the relationship is another. Of course, this has to do with the unity of knowledge and action.

5. How does love survive reality?

Love cannot only happen in the spiritual world.

Of course two people can talk about philosophy, literature, trauma and the future until three in the morning, feeling that each other has reached the depths of their souls. But in-depth chat creates a sense of intimacy, not necessarily a reliable relationship.

One person understands me very well, but may not be suitable to live with me; another person is not good at talking about grand issues, but may bring me water when I am sick, and stay to finish my words after a conflict.

Self-disclosure makes people feel close, and shared responsibility gradually creates trust.

Love has a material foundation and a body

Real love also has to face money, city, distance, work, school, family, living space, housework and caring responsibilities.

Where two people live, who adjusts careers for whom, how much time and travel expenses it takes to meet each other, who does more housework, how both parties use their joint money - these questions are not suitable for writing love poems, but they will determine the shape of the relationship day by day.

Love happens physically, too. Sexual attraction, touch, fatigue, illness, aging, differences in desire, all enter into relationships. The body is not a express box containing the soul, which can be thrown away after using the packaging. People really become impatient when they are sleepy, they really need care when they are sick, and they may feel alienated if they are not touched for a long time.

Calling all of this “not spiritual enough” will only result in a love that is beautiful but no one can live in.

Of course, admitting that material conditions are important does not mean that if your income falls below a certain level, you are not worthy of love. It just reminds me that feelings cannot pay all the real costs for two people. Roses are very beautiful, but landlords usually don’t collect roses when the rent is due.

Responsibility is usually not very romantic

Responsibility is not “I am willing to sacrifice everything for you” which is suitable for shouting in the rain. This is very idol drama, but not very realistic. It more often manifests itself as something without background music: try to do what you promised and explain in advance when you can’t do it; don’t punish the other party with silence; be willing to come back and communicate after a conflict; admit that your behavior has indeed caused harm; don’t escalate every quarrel into a relationship life or death.

Romance often happens at night. Responsibilities usually come the next morning, in the form of remembering to buy medicine, take out the trash, buy breakfast, and finish what you left off last night. This doesn’t sound earth-shattering enough, but most things that can live for a long time are not earth-shattering every day. It was shaking every day, and the neighbors couldn’t stand it.

Responsibility does not equal redemption of relationship

After a person hurts the other person, he can apologize, change, and bear the consequences, but these cannot exchange for the other person’s forgiveness, nor can he ask her to come back, nor can he ask her to be responsible for witnessing “I have finally changed for the better now.” If my growth still requires her to come back for approval, then there may still be an old account hidden in this growth: I have changed, so you should choose me again.

But the relationship is not about make-up exams. You don’t have to retake the wrong question and ask the same person to reissue it.

A more radical responsibility may be: I change the way I treat people in the future, but I don’t require the people I have hurt to prove that I have changed. Some repairs can only happen within myself, and the relationship itself will not be restored; nor is she a teaching aid specially sent to teach me how to grow. She is a person who has been truly affected, hurt, and has the right to close the book.

6. The minimal structure of mature love

Writing here, it seems that I can try to summarize it.

Of course, the discussion here is not about whether unrequited love counts as love, nor is it about giving a certificate to everyone’s relationship. All I want to ask is: What are the minimum requirements for a mature, two-way love relationship?

The first is speciality.

Indeed, love begins with mutual attraction and requires this motivation; love also supports both parties to gain something from the relationship and be willing to give. But as to why I only fall in love with him and why he is special, it is because I care about this specific person, not any substitute for the function she provides. Shared history makes her irreplaceable.

Second, it is two-way subjectivity.

Both people have the right to express, refuse, change and leave. Love cannot exist only in one person’s grand narrative and then require another person to co-star.

Third, it is trust that has been tested by reality.

Not only were we able to talk about souls, but we also faced time, fatigue, conflicts and interests together. Whether words and deeds are consistent, whether promises can be fulfilled, and whether injuries can be repaired are slower and heavier than a night of deep chatting.

Fourth, is responsibility for each other’s consequences.

Her happiness, pain, and dignity would truly influence my choices. I knew I had the power to hurt her, and I restrained myself from using it. We may have conflicts, but we must also have the ability to re-understand, apologize, adjust and restore links.

Fifth, there is interdependence within boundaries.

We allow ourselves to need each other, but we do not require one person to take on the other person’s entire life; we allow “us” to form, but do not eat “me” and “you” together.

So now I probably understand love like this:

Love is two limited people with desires, needs and realistic conditions who get close to each other because of each other’s specialness; trust, attachment and a common history are formed in repeated interactions, and in intimacy they recognize that the other person is still a subject that cannot be swallowed up by oneself; ultimately, through responsibilities, boundaries and continuous choices, a “we” is formed that does not eliminate “I” and “you”, but truly exists.

There is no “always understanding” or “never wavering” in this definition. These words are too big and difficult to place in reality.

All it requires is that while the relationship is still there, I treat you as a concrete person and take seriously what my actions will do to you.

7. In the end, the words fall back on yourself.

At first, I imagined love as meeting someone special enough, and then naturally gaining stability, understanding, and mutual salvation.

After I actually entered into a relationship, I was a very difficult person to judge. Rather than finding a partner, it would be better to say that I found a mother, or a super wishing machine.

Now I am more willing to admit that love has mechanisms, exchanges, bodies, and realistic conditions. But these explanations cannot erase the meaning that a specific person once gave me.

Explainable does not mean digestible. She was once real, and her departure is also real.

As for the future, I probably need to do a few less ambitious things.

First, establish more fulcrums for your life. Study, work, friends, interests, body and one’s own order should all continue to exist. Not to act independent, but to not let your partner bear the weight of the world.

Secondly, increase real contact. You can’t keep waiting for yourself to become good enough and then expect love to knock on your door automatically. If a person doesn’t go out, it’s probably difficult for him to master the art of walking through walls.

Then, slowly form the “we”. Get along first to see if each other is comfortable; then experience together to see if trust grows; and finally talk about long-term integration. Don’t just meet someone and then secretly arrange for her to play roles in the next few decades before they even receive the script.

You also need to learn to express yourself directly. Instead of rehearsing an entire tragedy in your head based on the speed of responses, ask, “I’ve been a little uneasy with the lack of contact lately. I wonder where we are now.”

When a conflict occurs, first distinguish whether the other party needs companionship and solutions at the moment, or whether there is indeed a long-term conflict that cannot be avoided. Don’t hold a seminar when someone is crying, and don’t judge every mood swing as a sign that the relationship is about to die.

No more pain to prove affection. Insomnia, rumination, and loss of order in life may mean that love is very important, or it may mean that other fulcrums are falling. Pain deserves to be seen, but the sound of pain does not mean the love is right.

Finally, learn to accept being loved. Allow others to take care of me and not be in a hurry to pay off the debt; allow myself to have needs, and allow others to say they can’t do it.

These words are quite clear now. I don’t know how much I can really do in the next relationship. Theorists usually sit on the shore and talk about swimming in a clear and logical manner.

But probably still have to go into the water.

Love is probably when one person gradually enters the consciousness of another person, and finally a “we” grows between the two people. It’s just that this “we” cannot obtain the freehold property rights, nor can we in turn evict the two people.

I don’t know when it is love and when it becomes habit, debt or unwillingness.

Time may tell.

But time is never responsible for writing standard answers.
