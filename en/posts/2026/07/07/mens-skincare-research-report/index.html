Chapter 1: Introduction—Why Men Need Skincare

1.1 An Overlooked Basic Need

For a long time, Chinese society has treated skincare as the exclusive territory of women. An invisible wall stands between men and skincare, built jointly from the stereotype that “roughness is masculine” and a lack of knowledge about skincare products.

Yet the global market for men’s skincare is undergoing unprecedented growth. In 2023, the global market for men’s facial skincare exceeded 18 billion US dollars, while the Chinese market reached approximately 18 billion yuan.

The principal drivers of this growth are the visual turn of social media, the rise of the “male economy,” and pressure arising from professional and social situations.

1.2 Core Position

This report adopts three principles: scientific, systematic, and sustainable. All claims of effectiveness should be supported first by evidence-based medicine, while every stage should ask one question: “What do the distinctive properties of men’s skin mean here?”

Chapter 2: The Development of Skincare

Ancient Egypt: men and women used almost identical skincare products—castor and olive oils for moisture, and Kohl eyeliner for protection against ultraviolet radiation.

Ancient Greece and Rome: the public bathhouse sequence of “cold water → warm water → hot water → steam” formed a prototype for the modern skincare routine.

Ancient China: literati during the Tang and Song dynasties used facial preparations containing traditional ingredients such as Angelica dahurica and Sichuan lovage; Su Shi and others discussed formulas for such preparations. Bathing beans and various scented ornaments were popular among Song scholar-officials.

The twentieth-century “silent period”: the two World Wars reinforced the model of “men = roughness,” and skincare products came to be regarded as a sign of effeminacy.

The Japanese and Korean wave: Shiseido took the lead in launching a men’s skincare line during the 1990s. K-Beauty then carried men’s skincare to a new level.

The Chinese market: emergence in the 2000s → growth in the 2010s → rapid expansion in the 2020s.

Chapter 3: Skin Physiology and Medical Foundations—the Male Version

3.1 Structural Differences in the Skin

Indicator Men Women
Thickness of the dermis Approximately 20-25% thicker Thinner
Quantity of sebum 1.5-2 times greater Lower
Subcutaneous fat Lower Higher

Wrinkles appear later in men but are deeper, while collagen is lost at a steady rate. The sebaceous glands are stimulated by DHT and produce 1.5-2 times as much sebum as those of women. Daily shaving increases transepidermal water loss by 20-50%.

3.2 Skin Ageing—the Male Version

Extrinsic ageing is men’s weak point: they participate in more outdoor activity but use sunscreen at a far lower rate than women. Glycation causes the skin to become yellow and dull.

Typical signs include enlarged pores, a dull and yellow complexion, hollow eye sockets, and deep, coarse wrinkles.

3.3 A Care Path Designed for Men

Dimension Key Action
Correct shaving Warm towel → lubrication → shave with the grain → after-shave care
Moderate cleansing Avoid alkaline soap bases
Moisturising Choose lightweight moisturisers such as gels or lotions
Sun protection SPF30+ for daily life and SPF50+ outdoors
Regulation of metabolism Niacinamide to control oil; retinol against ageing

Chapter 4: Identifying Skin Type

4.1 The Four Everyday Categories

Oily (oil across the complete face) → dry (tightness and flaking) → combination (oily T-zone and dry cheeks) → normal (extremely rare)

Cleansing test, at no cost: wash the face with warm water and observe—oil after 30 minutes = oily; tightness after one hour = dry.

4.2 Distinctive Features of Acne in Men

Men produce 1.5-2 times as much sebum as women, and acne appears more often in the beard region. The vicious cycle between shaving and acne is a management problem specific to men.

Common triggers of adult acne include whey-protein supplements, stress, a high-GI diet, and masks.

Skin type should be reassessed every quarter.

Chapter 5: Aesthetics, Bearing, and Skincare Strategies

Type of Bearing Skincare Priority
Fresh and clean Oil control + moderate cleansing + basic moisturising
Rugged Moderate oil control + contour care + after-shave repair
Mature and steady Anti-ageing + sun protection + deep moisturising
Cheerful and athletic Antioxidants + sun protection + lightweight moisturising
Cool and refined Detailed care + pore management + brightening

Men’s central requirements when choosing products: Fast + Invisible + Effective.
Preferred texture: light and non-sticky > rapidly absorbed > no film-like feeling.
Preferred fragrance: fragrance-free > woody > citrus.

Chapter 6: Facial Preparation and Preconditions

The Correct Shaving Process

  1. Apply a warm towel to the face for 1-2 minutes to soften the hair
  2. Apply shaving cream for lubrication
  3. Shave with the grain to minimise irritation
  4. Shave against the grain only where necessary
  5. Rinse with cold water + apply an after-shave product

Facial Cleansing

The feeling of “scrubbing a plate” does not mean that the face is clean—tightness indicates that excessive cleansing has damaged the skin barrier.

Choose an amino-acid cleanser for everyday use and occasionally use a combined cleanser for deeper cleaning. Say goodbye to soap-based cleansers completely.

Chapter 7: A System for the Daily Skincare Routine

Core Principle

A routine that can be maintained is far better than one that is optimal only in theory.

The Basic Three-Layer Framework

Cleanse → Moisturise → Protect/SPF

Morning Routine

Water or a gentle cleanser → after-shave care if shaving → vitamin C serum, optional → moisturiser → sunscreen, the most important step

Evening Routine

Cleanse → after-shave care if shaving → active serum → moisturiser

Retinol is the first-choice anti-ageing ingredient for men. Begin at a concentration of 0.25%-0.3%, use it twice per week, and gradually build tolerance.

Advanced Active Ingredients

Goal Ingredient Starting Concentration
Oil control Niacinamide 2%-5%
Blackhead removal Salicylic acid (BHA) 0.5%-2%
Anti-ageing Retinol 0.25%-0.3%
Brightening Vitamin C 10%-15%
Repair Ceramide/B5 —

Chapter 8: Lifestyle—the Invisible Half of Skincare

Diet: high-GI foods are positively associated with acne. Whey protein increases acne in some users.

Fitness: the first fifteen minutes after exercise are the golden window for facial care.

Sleep: insufficient sleep → increased cortisol → overactive sebaceous glands + greater inflammation.

Smoking: produces a “smoker’s face,” with a dull, grey-yellow complexion. Within one week of stopping smoking, the complexion brightens; within three months, wrinkles decrease.

Chapter 9: A Practical Guide to Product Selection

The Core Logic of Selection

Ingredients > brand > packaging. Before every purchase, ask yourself: What does my skin need? What is the core ingredient? Can I continue using it?

Understanding the Ingredient List

Rule of decreasing quantity: ingredients are listed from highest to lowest content. The 1% threshold: preservatives are generally added at below 1%, so ingredients appearing before them have a concentration above 1%.

Brand Selection

Type Recommended Brands Suitable Users
Dermocosmetics CeraVe, La Roche-Posay, and Paula’s Choice Those focused upon ingredients
Men’s lines Lab Series and Biotherm Homme Those seeking convenience
Japanese Shiseido Men Those prioritising the experience upon the skin
Chinese brands Simpcare and Proya Those seeking value for money

Budget Distribution

Sunscreen (¥100-300/month) + serum (¥150-500/month) + savings on cleanser (¥50-150/month). The average daily cost is approximately ¥10-15.

Chapter 10: Decision Trees and Typical Cases

A 25-Year-Old Programmer with Oily Skin (¥143/month)

Morning: amino-acid cleanser → niacinamide → lightweight lotion → sunscreen
Evening: amino-acid cleanser → salicylic acid every other day → lightweight lotion
Weekly: clay mask upon the T-zone 1-2 times

A 32-Year-Old Office Worker with Combination Skin (¥280/month)

Morning: water → vitamin C 10% → light moisturising lotion → sunscreen
Evening: amino-acid cleanser → retinol 0.1-0.3% every other day → repairing cream

A Mature 40-Year-Old (¥480/month)

Morning: gentle cleanser → vitamin C → peptide eye cream → rich face cream → high-SPF sunscreen
Evening: cleanser → retinol 0.3-0.5% → peptide eye cream → repairing night cream

A 22-Year-Old University Beginner (¥80/month)

Buy only three products: amino-acid cleanser + moisturising lotion + sunscreen. The goal is to establish a habit, not to obtain the most extreme result.

Chapter 11: Standard Procedures for Handling Problems

Level Symptoms Response
L1 mild Slight stinging or redness Reduce frequency and strengthen moisturising
L2 moderate Widespread redness and stinging Stop active ingredients and use repair products only
L3 severe Swelling or blisters Seek medical care immediately

Situations in which a doctor must be consulted: severe nodular or cystic acne, a rash lasting more than two weeks, widespread urticaria, or changes in a mole according to the ABCDE rule.

Economic Observations

Between the factory and the shop, the price of a pair of glasses increases five to ten times. A reasonable transaction price for Zeiss or Essilor is 25%-30% of the advertised market price. Do not buy a package offering “free frames with the lenses”—the frames supplied cost less than 20 yuan.

Sustainability

Bio-based acetate, recycled titanium, and replaceable modular designs are emerging environmental trends. Old glasses can be donated through Lions Clubs International, retained as a spare pair, or resold.

Chapter 13: Conclusion and Programme of Action

Three Core Principles

  1. Sun protection is the only skincare behaviour supported by overwhelming evidence
  2. Consistent performance of three basic steps > an occasional ten-step routine
  3. Understand ingredients > worship brands > follow trends blindly

A 90-Day Beginner Programme

Stage Goal
Month 1 Establish the habit—complete the three basic steps every day without interruption
Month 2 Introduce vitamin C in the morning and retinol at night, beginning twice per week
Month 3 Observe and adjust—assess the effects and optimise the plan

Guide to the First Action

Decide today to “buy three products first”:

  1. Amino-acid cleanser (≤¥80)
  2. Lightweight moisturiser (≤¥100)
  3. Daily sunscreen SPF30+ (≤¥100)

Keep the total budget below ¥300—less than the cost of a dinner with friends.

Men’s skincare is not intended to turn you into somebody else, but to make you the best version of yourself. Skincare is a healthy habit, like brushing your teeth or taking a shower: it is basic maintenance of your body.

Beginning matters more than perfection, and persistence matters more than intensity.
