Hairstyle Research Report

Extending from historical origins to scientific foundations, and from aesthetic principles to practical operation, this report covers almost every aspect of hairstyles. The main text remains available as a Markdown version for online reading, while a compiled PDF can be downloaded below and at the end of the article.

Reading Guide

This report consists of twelve chapters and systematically reviews every dimension of hairstyles: from history to physiology, from aesthetics to practice, and from ideas to tools. It may be read from beginning to end or selectively according to the reader’s interests. The first five chapters focus upon fundamental theory, while the remaining seven place greater emphasis upon practical operation.

Hairstyle Research Report

Chapter One: A Brief History of Hairstyle Development

As an important part of human society and culture, hairstyles reflect not only changes in aesthetic ideas, but also historical information drawn from several dimensions, including politics, economics, religion, and technological development.

1.1 The Evolution of Hairstyles in the Ancient World

Changes in Chinese Hairstyles across the Dynasties

During the Shang and Zhou dynasties, the belief that “the body, hair, and skin are received from one’s parents and must not be damaged” was deeply rooted. Both men and women grew their hair long, arranged it in a bun, and fixed it with a hairpin. During the Qin and Han dynasties, men generally wore a bun beneath a crown, and crowns and headcloths became central marks of social identity and position. The Tang dynasty represented the high point of Chinese hairstyling. Women’s styles flourished in extraordinary variety: towering buns rose like clouds and were combined with magnificent gold and silver ornaments and decorative forehead flowers, expressing the graceful splendour of the Tang empire. The Song dynasty turned towards restraint and inward elegance. Cloth caps and the Dongpo hat became popular among men, while women’s styles placed greater emphasis upon flowing lines and overall harmony.

During the Ming and Qing dynasties, the Qing government imposed the “queue order,” compelling men to shave the front of the head and retain a braid. From the Republican period onwards, the movement to cut off queues after the 1911 Revolution marked the end of the feudal age, while new short styles such as the Zhongshan cut and the 70/30 side part quickly spread.

The Evolution of Western Hairstyles

The ancient Egyptians developed the culture of wigs to its furthest extent. In ancient Greece and Rome, hairstyles became closely connected with aesthetic ideals. During the Renaissance, golden curls were regarded as the standard of ideal beauty. The seventeenth and eighteenth centuries marked the peak of Western wig culture, when enormous, full wigs worn by men became the ultimate symbol of power and position. After the nineteenth century, the modern system of short hairstyles for men gradually took shape.

1.2 The Modern Hairstyle Revolution

1920s: slicked-back hair and side-parted pompadours became standard equipment for a gentleman.
1950s: the Pompadour became popular throughout the world under the influence of Elvis Presley.
1960s: the Beatles’ mop-top hairstyle broke the fixed assumption that “men must wear short hair.”
1970s-80s: the punk movement introduced the Mohawk, while disco promoted curls and high-volume styling.
1990s-2000s: simple styles such as spiky cuts and crew cuts returned, while fade techniques matured.
2010s to the present: the Undercut spread across the world, and men’s hairstyling entered an “age of precision.”

1.3 Contemporary Hairstyle Trends

Popular trends for men: the fade remains the technical core; the Textured Crop is widely popular; curled fringes break the stereotype that Asian men must have straight hair; and the wolf cut is rapidly gaining popularity among younger people.

Popular trends for women: the wolf cut, butterfly cut, and Korean perming techniques such as the air-cushion perm, C-curl perm, and cloud perm remain highly fashionable.

1.4 Comparison between Eastern and Western Hairstyle Cultures

Eastern hairstyle culture pursues refinement and order, placing emphasis upon the texture and shine of the hair. Western hairstyle culture places greater weight upon volume and visible texture. Under globalisation, Eastern and Western hairstyle cultures are blending at an accelerating pace.

Chapter Two: Hair Physiology and Medical Foundations

2.1 The Anatomical Structure of Hair

A complete strand of hair contains three layers from the outside in: the cuticle (the outermost layer, consisting of 5-12 layers of scale-like keratinised cells), the cortex (more than 90% of the strand, determining mechanical strength and colour), and the medulla (the central layer).

2.2 The Hair-Growth Cycle

Stage Duration Proportion
Anagen 2-7 years 85%-90%
Catagen Approximately 2-3 weeks Approximately 1%
Telogen Approximately 3-4 months 10%-15%

2.3 Principal Factors Affecting Hair Quality

Factor Specific Effects
Genetics Colour, thickness, degree of curl, follicle density, and tendency towards hair loss
Nutrition Protein, iron, zinc, biotin, vitamin D, and Omega-3
Hormones DHT attacks hair follicles, mainly in men; thyroid hormone; cortisol
Environment and lifestyle Ultraviolet radiation, heated tools, chemical perming and colouring, sleep, and smoking

2.4 Androgenetic Alopecia

Androgenetic alopecia, or AGA, is the most common form of hair loss among men—affecting approximately 30% by the age of thirty, 50% by fifty, and as many as 80% by seventy. In essence, it is a process in which DHT causes the hair follicles to become progressively smaller.

The Norwood-Hamilton scale extends from Stage I, involving slight recession of the hairline, to Stage VII, in which only a horseshoe-shaped region of hair remains. Early intervention is critical. Drugs approved by the FDA include oral finasteride and topical minoxidil.

2.5 Common Problems of the Scalp and Hair

Problem Response Strategy
Androgenetic alopecia Finasteride for men, minoxidil, and hair transplantation
Seborrhoeic dermatitis Shampoo containing ketoconazole or selenium sulphide
Premature greying Primarily genetic; cover with hair dye

Chapter Three: Identifying and Classifying Hair Type

3.1 Four Basic Hair Types

Hair Type Typical Characteristics
Oily hair The scalp appears oily within half a day to one day after washing
Dry hair No oily feeling for 2-3 days after washing; prone to split ends
Normal hair A moderate amount of oil 2-3 days after washing
Combination hair Oily scalp but dry ends

3.2 Methods of Self-Testing at Home

  1. Blotting-paper test—measures the quantity of oil secreted
  2. Elasticity test—measures the elasticity of the hair shaft
  3. Float test—measures porosity
  4. Stretching test—measures breaking strength
  5. Comb test—measures the smoothness of the cuticle

3.3 Suitable Hairstyles for Different Hair Types

Hair Type Recommendations for Men
Oily hair Short textured cut, graduated short cut, or Undercut
Dry hair Short side part or classic pompadour
Fine, soft hair Keep the length below 5 cm and use volumising powder
Thick, coarse hair Heavily textured short cut or thinning to reduce weight

4.3 Hair Quantity and Choice of Style

Limited hair/receding hairline: a short Fade transfers visual attention towards the clean temples rather than the thinning top.

Thick hair: thinning or a textured perm reduces visual volume while increasing control.

Chapter Five: An In-Depth Analysis of Aesthetic Principles

5.1 Proportion and Mathematical Aesthetics

Three sections and five eye-widths: a hairstyle visually “redistributes” the proportions of the three regions of the face. A long upper section → fringe; a long middle section → broaden the sides horizontally; a long lower section → let the ends fall at the jawline or collarbone.

The golden ratio of 1:1.618: in hairstyle design, this appears across three principal dimensions—facial proportions, the relationship between hairstyle height and facial width, and the ratio of a side part.

Crown ratio and the effect of “the head enclosing the face”: when the crown ratio is at least 1/3, the face is visually “contained” within the frame of the skull and therefore appears smaller. East Asian skulls tend to have flatter crowns; principal corrective methods include root perms and blow-drying techniques that create a higher crown.

5.2 Principles of Visual Design

The language of lines: horizontal lines convey stability, vertical lines confidence, diagonal lines movement, and curves softness.

Contrast in texture: high shine (oil-based pomade/vintage), natural finish (water-based pomade/everyday), and matte finish (hair clay/street fashion).

The law of volume: the essence of a Fade is the creation of an extreme contrast in volume. Low, Mid, High, and Skin Fades each produce a different degree of visual impact.

5.3 Gestalt Principles

Figure-ground relationship: the hairstyle is the “ground,” while the face is the “figure.” The hairstyle must not steal the scene.

Law of closure: the proportion of the outline is a hundred times more important than perfection in the details.

5.4 Colour Aesthetics

Guide to choosing hair colour:

  • Cool skin tone: cool brown, ash brown, or grey-purple shades
  • Warm skin tone: caramel brown, chestnut, or reddish brown
  • Neutral skin tone: almost every colour can be attempted

A special note for men: “cooling” the hair colour is the key to making Asian men’s skin appear lighter—the blue tones in natural black hair complement yellow skin.

5.8 Differences between Eastern and Western Aesthetics

Eastern aesthetics: an aesthetics of concealment, including fringe culture, thick hair, and a full outline. The preference for a high crown in East Asia has roots in physiognomy—the “full heavenly court,” or upper forehead, is regarded as a sign of great fortune.

Western aesthetics: an aesthetics of exposure, displaying the complete hairline and the bone structure of the face.

5.10 Core Aesthetic Decision Tree

Begin with the question, “What do you want people to notice?” Eyes → open the face; facial outline → expose the forehead + jawline; overall bearing → unify the style; something you do not want noticed → conceal it selectively.

Chapter Six: Aesthetic Methods Related to Hairstyles

6.1 The Art of Coordinating Hairstyles and Glasses

Cat-eye/upswept frames Echo the structure Side-parted pompadour

Wellington/browline frames Classic gentleman Side-parted pompadour or Pompadour

6.2 Hairstyles and Accessories

Hats, earrings, and beards—the relationship between each accessory and the hairstyle determines the overall impression. Coordination between beard and hairstyle is an exceptionally important part of male aesthetics.

6.3 Choosing Hair Colour

Hair colour is the most rapidly changing and immediately influential dimension of hairstyle aesthetics. The central principle is compatibility with skin tone: keep the hair colour and skin tone within the same colour-temperature system.

Chapter Seven: How Hairstyles Modify Head Shape and Attractiveness

The central function of a hairstyle is to optimise facial proportions by reshaping the outline. A good hairstyle can make the face look “one size smaller and one degree more three-dimensional.”

High forehead/receding hairline → use a fringe or side part for coverage. Flat head shape/low crown → use a root perm, or pull the roots in the opposite direction while blow-drying. Wide cheekbones → grow the sides below the cheekbones to soften their appearance.

Modification of the jawline: in men, the jawline is the part of the face with the strongest sexual characteristics. By treating the sides and adjusting volume on top, a hairstyle can make the jawline visible to exactly the right degree.

Chapter Eight: Preparation before Changing Your Hairstyle

Before entering a salon, complete the following tasks. Take five photographs in advance: front, left and right at 45 degrees, and left and right at 90 degrees. Collect 3-5 reference images, taking care to find models whose hair type and facial shape resemble your own.

When communicating with the stylist, do not merely say “cut it a little shorter.” State the desired length, fade height, whether thinning is required, and the position and length of the fringe.

  1. Discuss requirements → 2. wash hair → 3. cut hair (establish the outline before treating the details) → 4. shave the face/shape the eyebrows → 5. style

An Explanation of Men’s Perms

Type of Perm Effect Duration
Textured perm Natural slight curl 2-4 months
Root perm Increased volume 1-2 months

Daily Styling at Home

Choosing Styling Products for Men

Pomade: oil-based products hold firmly but are difficult to wash out; water-based products are easy to clean. Suitable for pompadours and side parts.
Wax: provides a matte finish and is suitable for natural texture. It is used most frequently in Japanese and Korean styles.
Clay: the most popular product in recent years. Strong hold + matte finish, suitable for short to medium-length hair.
Hair Spray: provides the strongest final hold.

Steps for Styling Men’s Hair at Home

Wash → press with a towel to absorb water → blow-dry to 80% while shaping → apply styling product in several small quantities → make precise adjustments → set with hair spray

The most common mistake is using too much product. Use an amount the size of 1-2 soybeans for short hair and 2-3 soybeans for medium-length hair.

Chapter Eleven: Supplementary and Extended Topics

11.1 Seasonal Hairstyle Strategies

Season Recommendations for Men
Spring Short side part or Crop Cut
Summer High Fade, Skin Fade, or Buzz Cut
Autumn Textured Crop or side-swept texture
Winter Medium-length hair or full texture

11.3 The Psychology of Hairstyles

“Hairstyle anxiety”: men experiencing hair loss score significantly lower in self-esteem than men of the same age without hair loss. “The confidence of a shaved head”: actively choosing to shave the head can increase feelings of confidence and control.

The “haircut effect”: changing one’s hairstyle after a breakup, change of job, or move is a ritual form of “self-reconstruction.”

11.4 The Scientific Selection of Hair-Care Products

Surfactants: sulphates clean strongly but easily remove too much oil; amino-acid surfactants are gentler.

Ingredients that genuinely prevent hair loss must be delivered through leave-on products—minoxidil and finasteride. Anti-hair-loss shampoos remain upon the scalp for only 1-3 minutes and therefore have limited effects.

11.5 Special Hairstyles and Services

Hair transplantation: FUE, which leaves no linear scar and costs 10-30 yuan per follicular unit, is the mainstream approach in the Asian market. SMP scalp micropigmentation: a non-surgical option that imitates the “dark stubble” left by a shaved head.

11.6 Future Hairstyle Trends

Short Korean textured styles will continue to lead Asian fashion. A wave of curled hairstyles for men is approaching. Gender-neutral hairstyles are rapidly emerging among Generation Z. AI-assisted hairstyle design is moving from concept towards reality.

Conclusion

The best hairstyle is neither the most popular nor the most expensive, but the one most suitable for you. It should fit the condition of your hair, match your facial and head shapes, and become part of your way of life.

Hairstyles can be changed, aesthetic judgement can be cultivated, and one’s image can be improved. Every occasion on which you sit in a barber’s chair is an opportunity to understand yourself again.
