Building a Custom Huawei Tablet Theme from Scratch: HWT Dynamic Lock Screen and Home-Screen Wallpaper

MatePad Air · HarmonyOS 4.2 · 2800 × 1840
Beginning with an illustration I liked, I produced a native .hwt theme that Huawei’s Themes app could recognise, install, and use normally.

0. Why I Made a Huawei Theme Myself

The reason was simple:

Themes in Huawei’s store are far too expensive, and I found very few that genuinely appealed to me.

Many themes are essentially new arrangements of wallpapers, icons, fonts, and lock-screen layouts, yet they are not cheap. Worse, even after paying, I would often have to accept elements I did not want: enormous type, gaudy icons, peculiar lock-screen layouts, or wallpaper compositions unsuited to a tablet in landscape orientation.

In that case—

why not simply make one myself?

My requirements were clear:

  • Use a two-character illustration I liked on the home screen;
  • Place one character on each side and reserve the centre for icons and widgets;
  • Compose the lock screen separately instead of forcing it to share the home-screen image;
  • Put the time in the empty central region;
  • Preserve Huawei’s native lock screen, notifications, password, fingerprint, and unlocking logic;
  • Package the result as a real .hwt theme rather than installing a third-party “fake lock screen” app.

That decision led to this project.

The final result was:

Quiet Pair · Clear Clock v5

The home screen uses what I consider the strongest v3 composition, while the lock screen uses v2, whose characters are positioned more naturally. Both were adapted to the native 2800 × 1840 landscape and 1840 × 2800 portrait resolutions of my MatePad Air.

The most interesting part was not merely changing a wallpaper. It was gradually discovering how an HWT theme actually works under HarmonyOS 4.x.

1. Final Result

The final configuration:

  • Device: HUAWEI MatePad Air (DBY2-W00)
  • System: HarmonyOS 4.2
  • Landscape resolution: 2800 × 1840
  • Portrait resolution: 1840 × 2800
  • Home-screen wallpaper: v3
  • Lock-screen wallpaper: v2
  • Lock-screen clock: rendered in real time by the system DateTime component
  • Theme format: .hwt
  • Final version: v5 VectorClock

For the home screen, I moved the two characters towards the left and right, deliberately leaving a broad central region. Folders, icons, and widgets can therefore occupy the centre without covering either character.

The lock screen did not continue with the v3 home-screen composition, but returned to v2.

The reason is straightforward:

The home screen and lock screen perform entirely different visual tasks.

The home screen must remain usable, so the centre must accommodate objects. A lock screen is closer to a complete illustration and needs only to avoid the clock, date, and notifications.

I therefore stopped insisting that one image should serve every purpose:

Use the image best suited to the home screen there, and the image best suited to the lock screen on the lock screen.

The result immediately looked more natural.

2. Why I Did Not Use a Third-Party Lock-Screen App

At first, I considered finding a third-party application.

Such software, however, generally places another interface over the system lock screen, producing:

Attractive third-party lock screen
↓

↓

↓

The classic double lock screen.

A third-party application cannot truly replace HarmonyOS password, fingerprint, or security authentication either.

I therefore chose the native Huawei HWT theme route.

HWT determines only how the lock screen looks. HarmonyOS continues to handle security, notifications, and unlocking.

That division of responsibility is precisely what makes this route comfortable.

4. What Is an HWT?

Once this point is understood, the production process becomes much simpler.

An .hwt is not a mysterious binary format. It is essentially:

Theme resources arranged according to Huawei’s directory rules, plus XML configuration files, compressed as a ZIP and renamed with the .hwt extension.

Four sections matter most:

description.xml

The metadata of the theme: its name, author, version, and so forth.

unlock/

The part that controls the dynamic lock screen. Its XML specifies:

  • The background image;
  • The position of the clock;
  • The position of the date;
  • The unlocking region;
  • Separate landscape and portrait layouts.

wallpaper/

The home-screen and static lock-screen wallpapers.

preview/

The cover and preview images displayed by the Themes app.

I later discovered that “the lock-screen XML can run” and “the Themes app is willing to recognise the HWT” are separate questions. The latter also depends on metadata, previews, directory structure, and even caching.

More on that trap later.

18. What I Actually Learned

In retrospect, the project was much more interesting than changing a wallpaper. At least five things became clear.

First, Rendering and Recognition by the Themes App Are Different

Correct lock-screen XML does not guarantee that HarmonyOS will regard the .hwt as a complete theme.

Second, Adaptation Is Not Merely Changing Image Resolution

Real adaptation means treating all four situations separately:

It is not a matter of using one image everywhere. Whether an image looks good depends upon its use.

Third, Let the System Render Whatever It Can

Beautiful PNG digits still face resolution limits on a large screen. By contrast,

lets the system generate the text in real time.

Fourth, an HWT Already Working on the Device Is Extremely Valuable

Official documentation says what the correct theoretical structure is. A working theme says what this particular machine actually accepts. Combining the two is far more efficient than guessing.

Fifth, Change Only One Variable in Each Test

The Themes app has a cache. If wallpaper, XML, metadata, and directories all change before a test, a success or failure reveals nothing about its cause.

20. One-Page Checklist

  • Device model, system version, and resolution confirmed
  • Home and lock screens composed separately
  • Landscape and portrait wallpaper dimensions correct
  • description.xml encoded as UTF-8
  • unlock/theme.xml points to lockscreen
  • Every src in the manifest exists
  • Clock component deliberately chosen as Time or DateTime
  • preview/ exists
  • wallpaper/ exists
  • unlock/ exists
  • No extra directory level in the archive
  • File is not .hwt.zip

  • New version uses a new file name
  • Themes app cache cleared
  • Landscape checked
  • Portrait checked
  • Notifications checked

Conclusion

It began because the theme store was expensive and offered almost nothing I genuinely liked.

From changing a wallpaper, I moved into HWT directories, the HarmonyOS Theme Engine, dynamic lock-screen XML, landscape and portrait adaptation, PNG clocks, and DateTime text rendering.

Having made the entire route work, however, new themes will be much easier.

The project left behind not only the Quiet Pair · Clear Clock theme, but also a reusable process:

Consult official documentation to determine capability boundaries; examine an HWT verified on the actual device to confirm compatibility; adjust composition and coordinates through screenshots; finally, test through explicit versions.

Changing the wallpaper, characters, clock, date format, or entire visual style no longer requires starting from zero.

Mm.

If Huawei’s theme store still has nothing I like—

I will make it myself.
