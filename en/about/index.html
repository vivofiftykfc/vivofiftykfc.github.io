INTRODUCTION

I am an engineering student majoring in electronic information, and I hope to work on embodied intelligence in the future. I have experimented with radar, software development, embedded systems, and machine learning. I also have a habit of borrowing ideas from my courses and using formulas, systems, and models to explain parts of life. My MBTI type is probably INTP. I am reading Wang Yangming, Laozi and Zhuangzi, Nietzsche, and others.

I am good at drawing diagrams for things, finding causes for failures, and writing a perfectly structured manual for a life that has not yet begun, in the hope that I might follow it and become better. I once assumed that if I could explain something clearly, I had in some sense taken possession of it. After colliding with reality several times, I discovered that even the most elegant model remains a model: I still answer questions incorrectly, events still develop in unexpected ways, and maps always become outdated.

This blog began as a place for technical notes, film reviews, and miscellaneous thoughts. Gradually, it became something like a diary of the years around my twentieth birthday. Anxiety, emptiness, heartbreak, friendship, action, ambition, and those moments at two in the morning when I suddenly believed I had touched the foundations of the universe have all been placed inside these Markdown files. They helped me think through many things that had remained confused. They also made me rather skilful at substituting “I have understood it” for “I have done it.” In any case, I intend to stop writing for a while, enter a broader reality, and write the next articles upon the ground itself. Perhaps I will return after the postgraduate entrance examination. These earlier posts should at least allow some later visitor to see what I was doing when I was twenty.

I like Wang Xiaobo and would like to become someone like him: to play with dry humour, hide something serious beneath it, write brief essays and slightly sentimental pieces, and live a more interesting life. I like Wang Yangming and even enjoy the feeling of being scolded by him—although I am not McDonald’s. Somehow all the people I like here belong to the Wang family. Heh. I am also reading Kant, trying to discover how far human reason can travel and how much of the world we see belongs to the world itself. I find a great deal in Wittgenstein that resonates with me, and I rather like the phrase “language game.”

I still want to start a company someday and build things that people can genuinely use and test—things capable of changing something for particular human beings. Leaving a mark upon the world would of course be good; a flying goose leaves a trace. But if I cannot, I must first understand the questions, projects, body, and days immediately before me. Failing that, I can at least make this life more interesting. Let the grand things provide direction in the distance, while the road beneath my feet is travelled inch by inch.

For a shorter introduction organized more like a map of completed work, visit my portfolio. To see how these ideas emerged through repeated collisions, browse the posts under “Reflections.” That is about it.

INTRODUCTION

The immediate reason for building this blog was a meal with a senior student some time ago, during which I saw the personal blog he had made. I already knew that the relevant open-source projects were mature and relatively easy to use. Then the idea struck me, and I started building—not quite as casually as that sounds.

It also solved a long-standing problem: I had nowhere suitable to share things. I wanted to write more—to record the interesting little objects and systems I had experimented with, the works of art I had appreciated, and my thoughts and feelings about life. While I still had the time, I wanted to write longer pieces in order to protect my ability to think and express myself, rather than becoming lost in the storm of short video. But where could I put them? I did not particularly like the atmosphere on Tieba, Zhihu, or Xiaohongshu, and had no desire to armour every sentence merely to suit the local mood. QQ Zone and WeChat Moments were ill suited to long arguments; a short paragraph could neither satisfy my desire to speak nor explain what I meant, and my contacts might not want to read it anyway. Producing videos would consume still more time and introduce the same problem of pleasing a community.

A personal blog met the need rather well. Here I can say whatever I want, state what I think, and record what I wish to remember. After all, I am largely writing for myself, and almost no one visits to read these articles. Besides, it is an interesting little project, is it not? The project may be open source, but that does not prevent me from learning something about the front-end system along the way.

The final paragraph of this preface is for any reader who happens to arrive. If you would also like to build such a personal blog, look for the post in which I describe this site’s construction and the writing workflow I recommend.

Someone like me can probably only talk to himself here. Saying too much in front of everyone would do nobody any good.
